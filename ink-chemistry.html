01

Comprehensive Offset Ink Architecture

High-Viscosity Lithographic Paste & Composition Mechanics

Unlike low-viscosity flexographic or gravure inks, offset printing lithographic ink is a heavy, high-viscosity paste engineered to function on the fundamental principle of oil and water repulsion.
(फ्लेक्सो या ग्रैवॉयर के विपरीत, ऑफसेट इंक एक भारी हाई-विस्कोसिटी पेस्ट होती है जो ऑयल और वॉटर रिपल्शन के सिद्धांत पर काम करती है।)

1. Color Pigments (15% - 25%)

Provides optical density, hue, and tinting strength. Uses organic pigments (Phthalocyanine Blue, Quinacridone Magenta) and inorganic types (Carbon Black).

2. Resins & Binders (30% - 45%)

Rosin-modified phenolic resins or hydrocarbon resins. Binds pigment particles together and delivers exceptional gloss upon surface drying.

3. Solvents / Mineral Oils (30% - 40%)

High-boiling point petroleum distillates imparting proper press-roller fluidity and facilitating paper capillary penetration.

4. Driers & Additives (2% - 5%)

Cobalt, Manganese, and Zirconium metallic salts acting as catalysts to accelerate oxidation polymerization.

02

Rheology, Viscosity & Thixotropic Behavior

Shear Thinning Dynamics & Zometer Tack Sequencing

2.1 Thixotropy Mechanics (थिज़ोट्रॉपी का विज्ञान)

Offset inks exhibit thixotropic behavior: high viscosity at rest inside the fountain, and dramatic viscosity breakdown under high shear stress between high-speed rollers, returning to a firm state post-impression to prevent dot gain.

2.2 Zometer Tack Profiling (Heidelberg CX 104 Standard Sequence)

Tack values must sequentially decrease down the multi-color unit train to prevent back-trapping and blanket pulling:

Unit 1 (Black)14.0 - 16.0Highest Grip
Unit 2 (Cyan)12.0 - 14.0Controlled Peel
Unit 3 (Magenta)10.0 - 12.0Stable Transfer
Unit 4 (Yellow)8.0 - 10.0Lowest Tack

Emulsification Physics & Fountain Solution Parameters

Water-in-Oil Window, pH & Conductivity Control

3.1 Emulsion Window (18% - 25% Water)

Under-Emulsion (<15%): Ink becomes excessively tacky, leading to plate scumming and toning.
Over-Emulsion (>30%): Inverts to oil-in-water, causing dot spreading, dull prints, and roller starvation.

3.2 Fountain Solution Chemistry

pH Level: Strictly maintained between 4.8 to 5.2. Below 4.0 causes plate image wear and slow drying.
Conductivity: Range of 800 to 1200 µS/cm measuring dissolved ionic salts and gum arabic.

04

Drying Mechanics: Penetration and Oxidation

Two-Stage Curing & Polymerization Kinetics

Phase 1: Physical Penetration

Mineral oils penetrate paper capillaries immediately post-impression, setting the ink film and preventing stack set-off.

Phase 2: Oxidation Polymerization

Unsaturated fatty acids react with atmospheric oxygen ($O_2$), catalyzed by metallic driers, transforming into a hard protective film.

Advanced Press-Floor Troubleshooting Matrix

Expert Solutions for Heidelberg & Komori Presses

Defect Symptom Root Cause Analysis Supervisor Corrective Action
5.1 Picking & Fluffing High ink tack or weak paper surface pick resistance. Add gel varnish tack reducer, lower press speed, check chiller temp.
5.2 Roller Stripping Calcium glazing or fountain solution layer on steel/chrome rollers. Perform deep roller wash with calcium remover and copper/steel degreaser.
5.3 Ghosting Ink consumption/supply imbalance or incorrect nip pressure. Calibrate form roller stripe width (3mm–4mm); optimize fountain keys.
06

UV Offset Inks & Specialized Curing Chemistry

Free Radical Photopolymerization & LED-UV Systems

Free Radical Mechanism

UV-curable inks contain zero mineral solvents. Comprising monomers, oligomers, and photoinitiators, exposure to Mercury or LED-UV lamps triggers instantaneous photopolymerization within milliseconds, curing 100% on the substrate surface without penetration.

07

Pigment Dispersion, Wetting & Particle Size Dynamics

Aggregates, Three-Roll Mill & Micro-Scale Physics

7.1 Agglomerate Breakdown

Pigments form tight agglomerates during manufacturing. Inadequate three-roll mill wetting results in speckles and severe print graininess.

7.2 Particle Size (0.1 - 1.0 Micron)

Sub-micron particles increase tinting strength and transparency, whereas larger particles enhance opacity at the expense of gloss.

08

Advanced Drying Defects: Chalking & Crystallization

Inter-deck Adhesion Failures & Porosity Analysis

8.1 Chalking (चॉकिंग)

Overly porous stocks absorb all vehicle oil instantly, leaving dry un-bonded pigment powder on the surface. Fix: Use sealed inks or body varnishes.

8.2 Crystallization (क्रिस्टलाइजेशन)

Over-dried primary ink films form a glossy, glass-like barrier rejecting subsequent wet overprints. Fix: Control inter-deck drying times.

09

Densitometry, Dot Gain & Print Contrast Mathematics

Solid Ink Density (SID) & TVI Calculations

9.1 Solid Ink Density (SID Formula)
Density = log10 (1 / Reflectance)

Targets: Cyan (1.30-1.45), Magenta (1.30-1.45), Yellow (1.00-1.10), Black (1.60-1.80).

9.2 Dot Gain (TVI Formula)
Dot Gain (%) = Printed Dot (%) - Plate Dot (%)

Optimal limits: Highlights (5-25%), Midtones (10-15%).
